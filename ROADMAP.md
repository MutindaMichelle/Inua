## Daily discipline (what your course is checking for)

- Commit at the end of every working session, even a small one — "meaningful and well-structured commits that clearly reflect the work being done" is graded, so `git commit -m "wip"` repeated 40 times looks worse than fewer, clearly-described commits.
- Open the GitHub issue *before* you start the matching piece of work, not after — that's what "tangible development progress" checks are actually looking for during sprint reviews.
- If a day's scope turns out too big, split the issue rather than silently doing less — this keeps the milestone's progress bar honest.

## If you fall behind

Cut scope in this order, not by working faster: (1) Admin analytics/reports — mentee and mentor flows matter more for a demo. (2) Mentor quality profile trend chart — a number is enough for a prototype. (3) Styling polish on wireframe-derived screens — functional beats pretty for submission. Do **not** cut: the prediction endpoint, and at least one working screen per actor — those are what a marker will actually click through.
